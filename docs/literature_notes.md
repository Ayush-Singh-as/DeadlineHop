# Research grounding

1. Shangxing Wang, Hanpeng Liu, Pedro Henrique Gomes, and Bhaskar Krishnamachari. *Deep Reinforcement Learning for Dynamic Multichannel Access in Wireless Networks*, 2018. [arXiv:1802.06958](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06958). Supports partial selected-channel feedback, action/outcome histories, DQN, and correlated channels. Our queue/deadline/retune model is an extension; our experiments are synthetic and do not reproduce its recorded RF trace.
2. Serkan Ak and Stefan Brüggenwirth. *Avoiding Jammers: A Reinforcement Learning Approach*, 2019. [arXiv:1911.08874](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08874). Motivates temporal memory under uncertain interference. Its radar sees noisy all-channel information and its training task differs from ours. It does not establish an LSTM advantage for our observation space.
3. Yifan Wang, Xin Liu, Mei Wang, and Yu Yu. *A Hidden Anti-jamming Method Based on Deep Reinforcement Learning*, 2020. [arXiv:2012.12448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12448). Motivates action-dependent interference. Its spectrum/SINR/correlation reward and learned jammer are not our ACK-only, causal scripted-follower model. We do not feed jammer actions into the policy or reward shaping.
4. Hado van Hasselt, Arthur Guez, and David Silver. *Deep Reinforcement Learning with Double Q-learning*, 2015. [arXiv:1509.06461](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.06461). Basis of separate online action selection and target-network value evaluation.
5. Matthew Hausknecht and Peter Stone. *Deep Recurrent Q-Learning for Partially Observable MDPs*, 2015. [arXiv:1507.06527](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.06527). Optional future recurrent extension; no recurrent model is included in the core results.

The literature motivates RL as a sequential approach to uncertain dynamics and delayed consequences. It does not prove that RL must beat every conventional controller. Our main question is empirical comparison against adaptive baselines under deadlines and retuning overhead.
